# Penance

**Part II of the Boogeyman series**
by [gon pulvo](https://gonpulvo.com)

*Penance* is the companion story to *Boogeyman*.

In *Boogeyman*, I wrote a story about getting trapped in my own dream and creating something powerful enough to help me escape it.

That worked.

Sort of.

*Penance* is about the cost.

What happens when I can alter this world, but everyone living inside it has their own life, history, and reasons for wanting things to stay the way they are?

And if I keep "fixing" things, at what point am I the problem?

---

## About the Project

*Penance* is an experimental electronic album and narrative project built through M8 Community Jam prompts, dream logic, dialogue, environmental storytelling, and sound design.

Like *Boogeyman*, the story is nonlinear. Some events are direct experiences. Others are memories, projections, possibilities, or things that only make sense once you have more context.

The central idea is pretty simple:

**Every solution costs something.**

# Characters

## Creator

Me, more or less.

I'm the dreamer and original creator of this world. I still want to wake up, but that becomes a much harder thing to justify once the world stops looking like disposable dream scenery.

## Squid

A version of me I created to survive *Boogeyman*.

He's basically my ideals with fewer social or personal limitations. Determined, autonomous, creative, extremely capable, and occasionally way too comfortable solving problems permanently.

## Agnes

One of the few people who has enough history with this world to tell me when I'm full of shit.

She remembers events I don't, understands systems I barely know exist, and has her own reasons for not trusting everything I do.

## Oisin

A sorcerer who knows significantly more about magic than I do.

Through him, I start realizing magic isn't just "I imagined something and now it exists." There are rules, history, consequences, and people who were doing this long before I showed up.

## Pastor

A recurring figure whose identity becomes tangled up with the stranger mechanics of the dream world.

His existence raises some uncomfortable questions about how much a person can be changed before we're talking about someone else entirely.

## Cornflake

Squid's longtime friend and partner.

He's often standing close enough to Squid's decisions to experience the consequences firsthand.

---

# Magic

Magic in *Penance* isn't free.

Changing reality requires something from reality. The bigger the intervention, the bigger the potential consequence.

One idea I kept coming back to while writing this was **potentiality**:

> What possibilities disappear when one possibility is forced into existence?

So magic isn't really an unlimited resource. It's closer to messing with a system that already has things happening inside it.

# The Central Problem

I want to wake up.

Easy enough, except the dream now contains people with memories, relationships, ambitions, fears, and their own reasons for wanting to exist.

If I leave, what happens to them?

If I stay, what happens to me?

And if I change the system so everyone gets what they want, who's actually paying for that?

That's *Penance*.

---

# Power and Responsibility

Almost every major problem in *Penance* eventually turns into the same question:

**Who carries the burden?**

The robot sustains a civilization. Oisin manipulates life and death. I alter magic. Squid pursues autonomy. Agnes remembers what happened.

Nobody gets to stand completely outside the system.

---

# Rain

Rain becomes one of the major motifs in *Penance*.

For me, it's about movement and redistribution. Power that was concentrated somewhere begins circulating somewhere else.

But redistribution isn't deletion.

You move power, you change who has access to it.

Then you get to deal with whatever that causes.

---

# Limits

One thing I kept coming back to while writing *Penance* is that limits aren't necessarily bad.

If someone can do literally anything, there isn't much room left for anyone else to make meaningful choices.

So the question becomes less:

> How powerful can I become?

And more:

> How much power can I have without making everyone else's agency meaningless?
